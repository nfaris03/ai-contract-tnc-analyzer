# Week 1 – Problem Observation Sheet

## Project

AI-Powered Contract and Terms & Conditions Analyzer

## 1. Observation Focus

This observation sheet records the problem context that motivates the project. It is an initial problem analysis, not the result of a completed user interview or formal survey.

## 2. Observed Problem Context

People are often required to accept contracts, service agreements, and Terms & Conditions before using a service or completing a transaction. The documents can be lengthy and may use formal language. A user who wants to understand a particular condition may need to search through many sections manually.

Examples of conditions that may be difficult to locate or interpret include:
- Whether a subscription renews automatically.
- Whether cancellation must be completed before a specific date.
- Whether additional charges may apply.
- What happens after late payment or early termination.
- How personal information may be collected or shared.
- Which responsibilities or liabilities are limited.

These are examples of clauses to investigate, not claims that every document contains them or that every such clause is harmful.

## 3. Users and Their Needs

### Primary users
Consumers and general users who need to review an agreement before accepting it.

### User needs
- Find important conditions without reading every page manually.
- Understand complex wording in simpler language.
- See the original clause that supports an explanation.
- Know which points may require clarification.
- Understand that automated analysis can be incomplete or incorrect.

## 4. Current User Journey

1. A user receives or opens a contract or T&C document.
2. The user may skim the document or search for familiar keywords.
3. The user encounters formal or lengthy clauses.
4. The user may be unsure about the practical meaning of a condition.
5. The user decides whether to accept, reject, or seek clarification, potentially without fully understanding every clause.

This journey is a proposed scenario for analysis and should be validated with actual users if the course requires primary research.

## 5. Pain Points

- Time required to review lengthy documents.
- Difficulty understanding legal or formal terminology.
- Important conditions may be located in different sections.
- Keyword searches may not capture the full meaning of a clause.
- Users may not know which conditions deserve further attention.
- General-purpose summaries may omit the exact clause or its exceptions.

## 6. Desired Outcome

A user should be able to upload a supported document and receive an organised report containing:
- Selected clauses that may require attention.
- The category assigned to each clause.
- A preliminary review level with an explanation of uncertainty.
- A plain-language explanation grounded in the source text.
- The original clause for verification.
- Questions or points the user may want to clarify.

## 7. Stakeholder Impact

- **Consumers:** May gain a more accessible way to review conditions.
- **Small businesses and freelancers:** May use the tool to navigate agreements before seeking professional review.
- **Legal professionals:** May find clause navigation useful as a supporting aid, while retaining responsibility for legal interpretation.
- **Organisations publishing agreements:** May receive clearer questions from users about their terms.
- **Project team:** Must manage reliability, privacy, evaluation, and responsible communication.

## 8. Evidence Still Required

The following should be collected before treating the observations as validated findings:
- At least one or more user interviews, if required by the course.
- Examples of public contracts or T&C documents.
- Three checked existing solutions or research studies.
- Evidence supporting the selected problem and engineering gap.
- Public data-source details, including access and reuse conditions.

Do not report interviews, survey results, user quotations, or statistics unless they have actually been collected.

## 9. Initial Design Implications

- Preserve the source clause so users can verify the analysis.
- Avoid producing a risk label without explaining the reason.
- Separate clause category from severity.
- Display uncertainty and limitations clearly.
- Avoid retaining uploaded documents longer than necessary.
- Make the interface understandable to non-specialist users.
